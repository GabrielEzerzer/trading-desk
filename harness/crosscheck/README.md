# Cross-check against NautilusTrader

This harness runs the same strategy on the same bars in our engine and in [NautilusTrader](https://nautilustrader.io), then compares fills, fees, funding, round-trip trades, the equity curve and the headline metrics under the agreement rule of issue #29. Nautilus is only an independent reference for this harness. It is not a dependency of the engine or the apps, and the harness is not part of CI.

The ten cases of the issue's table are here: market orders, sizing, flips and funding in cases 1 to 4, and stops, limits, stop-limits, attached stops, cancels, ambiguous bars and missing data in cases 5 to 10.

## Layout

- `cases/caseNN.json`: one case each: strategy and parameters, venue, kind, symbol, timeframe, period, starting cash, maker and taker fees, and the export folder; `about`, a line on what the case runs into; `exercises`, the exercises of its row in the issue's table, as ids from `exercises.py`; and, where some are not exercised, `exercise_note`, why, and whether a period that exercises them was searched for.
- `../../crates/td-engine/examples/crosscheck.rs`: our side, a Rust example with three commands. `fetch` downloads a case's candles and funding with td-data into this folder's own store (`data/store`, never the user's default store) and exports the bars, the gapless bars and the funding to `data/export/caseNN/`. `run` runs a case through `td_engine::run` (or `run_strategy`, for the two strategies only the cross-check uses) and writes JSON. `metrics` computes our metrics report for any equity curve.
- `nautilus_side.py`: the Nautilus side: the same case on the same exported bars, with the strategies written again in Python.
- `compare.py`: the agreement rule. `tests/test_compare.py` tests it offline on small hand-made results; `tests/test_nautilus_fills.py` pins the Nautilus fill behaviours the rule relies on, on hand-made bars.
- `exercises.py`: counts the instances of each exercise in a case's run (`tests/test_exercises.py`).
- `run.py`: runs everything and writes `reports/caseNN.md`, which are committed. `tests/test_run.py` tests the causes it names for a failure.
- `data/` (market data) and `out/` (both engines' raw results, curves and metrics) are git-ignored.

## Setup

You need the repo's Rust toolchain and [uv](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/). From this folder:

```
uv sync
```

That creates `.venv` with Python 3.12 and the pinned `nautilus_trader==1.231.0` (about 500 MB).

## Running

One command runs every case: it builds our example, fetches any case whose export is missing, runs both engines on the gapless and the real bars, compares them and writes the reports.

```
uv run python run.py
```

- One case, or a few: `uv run python run.py --case case03 --case case04`.
- Download and export the data again: add `--fetch`. The first fetch of all ten cases takes several minutes and stores about 90 MB under `data/`.
- The comparison logic's tests: `uv run pytest`.

The exit code is 1 if any case is not PASS.

The downloads use the venues' public endpoints only (instrument lists, Binance's candle archive, Bybit's klines, both venues' funding history) through td-data's own client, which sends the plain `trading-desk` user agent and nothing that identifies anyone.

## Reading a report

Each report starts with the verdict, then the setup (data, dropped buckets, funding stamps), the exercises and two sections. The verdict is FAIL if any check fails; NOT EXERCISED if every check agrees but the run has no instance of one of the case's exercises, since agreement on something that never happened shows nothing; and PASS otherwise. The exercises section counts the instances of each exercise on the real bars, with where they are, and repeats the case's note on any exercise left without instances.

- **Gapless bars.** A copy of the bars with every open set to the previous close, so a market order gets the same price in both engines. Fills must pair one to one with the same bar, side and quantity (the bar is the decision bar for a fill at the price of its decision, and the bar a resting order fills on otherwise); prices within one tick; fees within 1e-9 relative; funding per bar within 1e-9 relative; the equity at every bar and at the end within 1e-6 relative. Round-trip trades must pair one to one by the decision bar of their entry fill and their side, close on the same decision bar (or both still be open at the end), and have the same quantity; entry and exit prices within one tick; fees within 1e-9 relative; P&L net of fees and before funding within 1e-6 relative; funding within 1e-9 of the total size of the charges that make it up. Trade fees and P&L may also differ by up to four units of the 8th decimal, the floor for a P&L near zero: Nautilus keeps money to 8 decimals and a trade's amounts pass through at most four roundings of half a unit (a flip's commission share, the rest of it, the exit commission, the realized P&L), so the floor is twice the worst case.

  Two kinds of fill are held differently. An *ambiguous bar* has two or more of our resting orders reachable inside its range, not at its open: a stop and a target, an entry and its attached stop, a buy limit below the open and a buy stop above it. Bars carry no order of events inside them, so which order the price met first is unknown; our broker fills the stop first, the worse case, and Nautilus walks open, high, low, close (expected difference 2). Fills on such a bar must still pair one to one, but not on price or fee, and each trade entered or closed on one must have our P&L at most Nautilus's. Its P&L difference is taken out of our equity from the trade's exit on, and the bars from an ambiguous entry to its exit are not compared, so the rest of the curve still meets the exact rule. The ambiguous bars are found from our engine's open orders before each bar, which the export includes. Each of our fills on an ambiguous bar must still be at the worse listed level for its side, within a tick: the highest for a buy, the lowest for a sell, and the next worse for a second fill of the same side. The other is the *stop-limit in Nautilus's bar walk* (expected difference 4): a stop-limit triggered inside a bar whose range runs past its limit fills at the trigger in ours, as a taker; Nautilus 1.231 triggers it at the high (a buy) or low (a sell) of its walk, finds the limit not marketable there, rests it, and fills it as a maker at the first later point of the walk where the limit is marketable, no better than the trigger. That is usually the trigger, but it can be the close, or a later bar. A fill is held this way only when Nautilus's walk puts it on the same bar as ours as a maker, and when our own fill follows our fill model within a tick, with our model's liquidity and its fee at that rate. Nautilus's fill must then be at the walk's price within a tick with the maker fee, and the difference between the walk's price and fee and our model's is taken out of our trade P&L and equity. A fill of ours off our own model is held by the exact rule.
- **Real bars.** Fills must pair one to one. A fill at the price of its decision must be within `|open[N+1] - close[N]|` of Nautilus's, N being its decision bar; a resting order's fill within one tick plus how far the bar opened through its level; a fill on an ambiguous bar is held only to the worse listed level for its side, as on the gapless bars. The final equity difference must stay within the sum of the bounds:
  - bound 1, `qty * |open[N+1] - close[N]| * (1 + taker)` for each fill at the price of its decision; the `(1 + taker)` factor is the fee charged on that price difference;
  - bound 2, for each ambiguous bar, `qty * (highest level - lowest level) + qty * highest level * fee spread`: the engines may fill different orders of the bar, so a price can differ by the spread of their levels and a fee by a maker fee against a taker fee on the dearest level. The fee spread is `max(|maker|, |taker|)` when the two rates have the same sign, since two fees of the same sign differ by at most the larger, and `|maker| + |taker|` when a maker rebate makes them differ in sign;
  - bound 3, `qty * |open - level| * (1 + taker)` for each resting fill the bar opened through: a limit (both engines fill it at its own price, where a venue would fill at the open), or a stop-limit filling at its trigger point, at the open in ours and at the trigger in Nautilus;
  - the stop-limit walk, for each fill held as expected difference 4, `qty * (|walk price - model price| + the dearer of the two * |our model's rate - maker|)`: the walk's part only, Nautilus's price against our model's price and the maker fee against our model's fee.

  Trades must pair one to one as on the gapless bars, and each trade's P&L (net of fees, before funding) must stay within the bounds of its fills at our quantity; a trade on an ambiguous bar must also have our P&L at most Nautilus's within the bounds of its other fills. Nautilus's P&L is scaled to our quantity first: the engines size from their own equity, so paired trades can differ by a lot, and that sizing difference (explained under the fills) is not a price difference bound 1 covers. The scaling is exact for a trade with one entry fill and one exit fill, which is every trade in the ten cases; a Nautilus trade with more fills fails the check until the bound is summed per fill.

A case with fills but no paired trades fails, as a case with no paired fills does.

On both series every trade's exported net P&L must equal its `gross_pnl - fees - funding` (funding paid is positive) within 1e-6 relative or the money floor, in both engines' exports. The other trade checks compare P&L before funding, so they would not see a wrong net figure.

Both sections end with the metrics computed with our formulas from each engine's equity curve. "Differences and their reasons" lists every difference the report found with its explanation; anything listed there as unexplained fails the case. A failure whose cause is known still fails, and the list names the cause and the checks that fail from it. Two causes are named so far: a stop-limit that Nautilus's bar walk fills on a later bar than ours (case 9), and fills our broker rounds to the instrument's current tick where the bars carry prices off it, found when every differing price is our model's price rounded to the tick against the order (case 6): on the gapless bars Nautilus's price rounded, on the real bars the price our fill model gives before the rounding, where the bars' prices are off the tick. Only the price-derived checks (prices, quantities, fees, trade amounts, equity and their bounds) are put on either cause; any other failing check is listed as unexplained.

## How the two engines are lined up

- **Bars.** Both engines read the bars exported from our store. Nautilus gets them as external LAST bars stamped at their close and matches each one open, high, low, close (its default ordering) before the strategy sees it. Funding stamps are snapped to the nearest bar boundary when exported.
- **Fills.** Slippage is 0 in both, and Nautilus runs with `FillModel(prob_fill_on_limit=1.0, prob_slippage=0.0)` and its default open, high, low, close walk of each bar. A market order decided at bar N's close fills at bar N's close in Nautilus and at bar N+1's open in ours, which is expected difference 1. A resting order (limit, stop, stop-limit, attached stop) fills inside a bar in both, stamped with the bar's open in ours and its close in Nautilus. Fills are therefore paired by bar, not by time: the decision bar for a fill at the price of its decision (our export marks those fills), the bar it filled on for the rest. An order decided on the last bar never fills in our engine, so the Nautilus strategies send nothing on the last bar.
- **Orders.** The Nautilus side keeps a mirror of our engine's open orders, with our ids, and sends every order through a port of `Context::submit`: the same rounding (quantity down to the lot, a limit price down for a buy and up for a sell, a trigger to the nearest tick) and the same checks. Three of Nautilus's ways are worked around so that it executes what our engine would. A protective stop attached to an entry is sent when the entry fills, for the filled quantity, as our broker places it then. A reduce-only exit decided together with its entry is held until the entry fills, because Nautilus rejects a reduce-only order while there is no position. An order sent after a cancel in the same decision is held until Nautilus acknowledges the cancel, in the same bar's processing, because Nautilus's cash account keeps the cancelled order's cash locked until then and would reject the new order on it, where our engine frees it at once.
- **Fees.** The case's maker and taker rates are set on the Nautilus instrument and charged by its default maker/taker fee model. Nautilus rounds a commission to the 8 decimals of its money type; a fee that differs by less than half of that unit is reported as explained.
- **Accounts.** Spot runs use a Nautilus cash account, perpetuals a margin account at 10x, which the strategies never come near. Equity on the Nautilus side is the account's USDT balance plus the base asset at the close (spot), or plus the open position's unrealised P&L from Nautilus's own position (perpetual).
- **Funding.** Nautilus 1.231 has no funding settlement for perpetuals in a backtest, so the harness charges it into the Nautilus account through `SimulatedExchange.adjust_account`, the hook Nautilus's own FX rollover module uses, at our engine's stamps and on our convention: a stamp at or before a bar's open is charged on the position held going into the bar at the open price, and a stamp inside the bar on the position after the bar's fills at the close. The position is Nautilus's own.
- **Trades.** Ours are the run's `trades` (`td_engine::trades::build_trades`). Nautilus's are its own positions, the rows of its positions report: the positions in the cache plus the snapshots a NETTING account takes of each closed position before it reuses the position ID. A position's entry and exit time are its opening and closing fills' times, its quantity the peak quantity, its prices Nautilus's average open and close prices, its fees its commissions, and its P&L Nautilus's realized P&L, which is already net of those commissions.
- **Flips.** A fill that takes the position through zero is one closed trade and one new trade in both engines: the open quantity closes the old trade at the fill's price, the rest opens the new one at the same price, and the fill's fee is shared between them in proportion to those quantities. Nautilus's execution engine splits the fill that way on a NETTING account (`_flip_position`), and our trade builder does the same, so no conversion is needed; the comparison pairs the new trade by the flip fill's decision bar.
- **Funding in trades.** Nautilus has no funding of its own, so its realized P&L excludes it, and the trade P&L is compared before funding on both sides (`gross_pnl - fees`). Funding is compared per trade on its own, on the gapless bars: ours is the trade builder's (funding at a bar's open to the trade held into the bar, later funding to the trade open after the bar's fills); on the Nautilus side the strategy records which Nautilus position each harness charge was made on, by the position's opening time, and a trade's funding is the sum of those charges. On the real bars funding per trade is not compared, since the positions differ in size; the totals are reported.
- **The end of the run.** Our engine runs here without the end-of-run close-out (`close_at_end: false`), so no trade is force-closed. A position still open after the last bar is reported by our engine as an open trade marked at the last close, without an exit fee, and Nautilus leaves its position open too. The Nautilus side reports that open position the same way: exit at the last bar, at its close, with P&L equal to its realized P&L (the entry commission) plus its unrealized P&L at that close. A trade open at the end in one engine but closed in the other fails the pairing.
- **Strategies.** `sma_cross` is our registered strategy. Buy and hold and short and hold (`crosscheck_buy_and_hold`, `crosscheck_short_and_hold`) exist only in the example, so they never appear in the apps' strategy list: each sends one market order at the first bar's close for `fraction` of equity net of the taker fee, then holds. `donchian_breakout` is registered too. Four more exist only in the example, for the order types: `crosscheck_limit_revert` (a limit entry below the close and a limit exit above the entry, each cancelled after N bars), `crosscheck_bracket` (a market entry on an SMA cross with an attached stop and a reduce-only target limit, ATR multiples away), `crosscheck_stop_limit_breakout` (a stop-limit entry above a channel high in an uptrend, cancelled after N bars, closed at the market after a fixed hold) and `crosscheck_sma_limit` (the SMA cross entered and exited by a limit near the close, cancelled after N bars). The Python versions repeat the Rust decisions step by step, including the rounding down to the lot and the order checks our engine makes at submission (minimum size and notional). They sum moving averages in a plain loop because Python's `sum` compensates rounding and can differ in the last bit.

## Adding a case

Write `cases/caseNN.json` like the others and run `uv run python run.py --case caseNN`. A new strategy needs a Rust version (registered, or in the example's `harness_strategy`) and a Python one in `CrosscheckStrategy.decide`, sending its orders through `send` and `cancel` so the order mirror stays in step.
